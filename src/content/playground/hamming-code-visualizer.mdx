Hamming codes are the standard first answer to a real question: a bit flips in
transit, and the receiver has to notice — and ideally fix it — without asking for
the message again.

The maths is short enough to fit on a slide, which is exactly the problem. You
can follow the derivation and still not have any feel for *why* the parity bits
sit at powers of two, or how the failing checks add up to the position of the
corrupted bit. That is the thing this visualiser is for.

## What you can do with it

- Enter a message and watch the parity bits get placed and computed.
- Flip any single bit, as a transmission error would.
- Watch which parity checks fail, and see their positions sum to the index of
  the bit that changed.

That last step is the whole idea. The failing checks are not a lookup table —
their positions are the binary representation of where the error is. Once you
have watched it happen a few times, the placement at powers of two stops looking
arbitrary.

## Why it exists

The step everyone gets stuck on is not the encoding — it is believing that the
syndrome really does point straight at the broken bit. Watching it happen is
faster than being told, and it is the kind of thing I would rather build than
re-read.
